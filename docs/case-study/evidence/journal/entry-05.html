Independent review (GLM), round 1: FAIL. One blocker, and it was real. The coordinator read the layout, spent seconds creating windows, then wrote the whole tab map back. If you ran hermes-tmux open in that gap, the write erased your tab’s record and the next pass opened a second window for the same worker. Fix: the pass remembers what it read and merges only its own changes. A test places the race exactly and fails if the merge is replaced by the old wholesale write.

Round 2: PASS, no blockers. GLM traced the merge through four interleavings. It also withdrew one of its own round-1 claims after I asked it to settle it by experiment: it had said a pane you split inside one of our windows inherits our marker and would be closed by down. On a private tmux server the new pane has no marker of its own. My code comment said the same wrong thing; corrected to what was measured.

Round 2 notes I took.
  • Running hermes-tmux tail --no-follow by hand left your shell without echo. Now echo is only turned off while following and is put back on exit.
  • One failed tmux call could make a healthy tab be forgotten. A “no” is now asked twice; no answer at all leaves the tab alone.
  • open and the coordinator creating the same worker’s first tab at once left two windows. The recorded one stands; the other is closed.
  • A pane you move into your own window with join-pane is forgotten, left running, and nothing is written to your window.
Defects the DeepSeek workers found while writing tests (reported, not hidden): a clipped transcript line could overflow a narrow pane and cut its own “(+N chars)” marker; a lone closing line printed “exit: completed”; a one-shot read hid an unterminated last line (a worker test had asserted that behaviour, I changed the behaviour and the test); the first delegation on a fresh profile was only noticed on the slow 20 s wake.
